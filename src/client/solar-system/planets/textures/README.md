# Texture tier selection

Which rung of a body's texture ladder the renderer holds, decided from the
live viewport every frame. The ladder itself is built by
`scripts/textures/` and its contract is `data/textures/README.md`
§ Artifact contract; `../planet-mesh-layer.ts` is the only consumer.

```
src/client/solar-system/planets/textures/
  texture-ladder.ts (+ test)      The selection rules, and the accessors
                                  over the generated table.
  texture-ladder-generated.ts     AUTO-GENERATED by build-textures.py:
                                  per-body rungs + the one mean luminance
                                  they share. Do not edit.
```

**Generated, not fetched.** Selection has to answer before the first map is
requested, so a manifest arriving over the wire would leave the first
approach picking a rung it cannot yet know exists. The table is small and
static, so it rides the bundle.

## The demand

```
requiredWidth = 2 · physicalPlanetSizePx · pixelRatio
```

Two texels of map width per device pixel, because an equirect map spans
360° of longitude across its width while the visible hemisphere is 180° —
so the disc diameter is covered by only `W/2` texels.

`physicalPlanetSizePx` is the body's TRUE projected diameter in CSS px, and
is deliberately the size accessor NOT gated on `drawCutoffMag()`. It already
folds in distance, viewport size **and** field of view, so driving selection
off it gets all three for free. `pixelRatio` is the one input it does not
carry; it is read live off the shared uniform the renderer itself draws at,
so a resize or a drag onto a different-DPR monitor re-selects without a
listener of its own.

## Three rules, and only the third is hysteresis

1. **Round up, never nearest.** The smallest rung at or above the demand,
   clamped to what the body actually ships — a body has no rung above its
   own master, and asking for one would 404 every frame. Venus tops out at
   1800 however close the camera gets.
2. **Lead the swap.** Move up at `TEXTURE_TIER_LEAD` (0.75) of the resident
   width, before the resident rung is actually outgrown. This is the rule
   that preserves the guarantee: waiting until the demand exceeds the
   resident width means the map is already soft at the moment the fetch
   *starts*, and its arrival then reads as a visible sharpening pop.
3. **Never downgrade.** Flying away, a narrower map would spend an upload to
   make the image worse. One-way ratcheting also makes thrash structurally
   impossible, which is why there is no matching down-hysteresis — release
   belongs to eviction, not to selection.

A rung is promoted to *drawn* only once it is fully resident — decoded,
uploaded, mips built. Swapping on first byte shows a frame of black or
bottom-mip, which is the pop these rules exist to avoid.

Selection is strictly better than one tier per device: most of the time a
body is nowhere near the camera floor, so even a 5K display holds a small
map for a distant Mars.

## The mean luminance must not move between rungs

`uSurfaceLuminance` is pre-divided by the map's sphere-weighted mean linear
luminance, so the brightness-stretched mosaic supplies only the albedo
pattern (`../emission/README.md` § Two disc means). If two rungs of one body
measured even slightly differently, **every tier swap would be a brightness
step** on a body whose magnitude is physically pinned.

Fixed at the source rather than damped: the mean is computed once at build
from the body's top rung, shipped per body in the generated table, and used
by every rung. That also deleted a per-load CPU pass over the decoded image.
The same reasoning applies to any future quantity derived from the image
rather than from physics.
